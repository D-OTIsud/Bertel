# Sustainability group authoring review — 2026-11-10

Scope: 76 native sustainability groups, with preferred labels and definitions in French, English, Spanish and German. The native source IDs, source kinds and codes are preserved. The nine native parent categories were read for context; no category, membership, hierarchy or flag is changed by this content file.

French labels and definitions are retained from the supplied native dictionary. Its definitions already explain the practices beyond their labels. English, Spanish and German texts were individually authored from those French meanings, including distinctions between information and services, feedback collection and analysis, waste sorting and composting, and water consumption versus water quality. No outside facts, legal thresholds, certification guarantees or verified outcomes were added. This is an authoring and semantic consistency review, not a claim of native-speaker validation.

## Wording choices to review

- `SA_CARBON_FOOTPRINT`: the French wording “trajectoire de réduction et de financement bas-carbone” leaves the financing object broad. Proposed translations retain a reduction pathway and financing of low-carbon solutions; they do not introduce offsets or carbon-neutrality claims. If a financing trajectory rather than a financing plan is intended, use “reduction and low-carbon financing pathway”, “trayectoria de reducción y financiación baja en carbono”, and “Reduktions- und Finanzierungspfad für kohlenstoffarme Lösungen”.
- `SA_COASTAL_WASTE_MANAGEMENT`: “nettoyage raisonné” is rendered as carefully planned / planificada y cuidadosa / bedarfsgerecht geplant. This retains considered management without inventing a particular beach-cleaning protocol. A more ecological intent would need source clarification before adding it.
- `SA_DIGITAL_SOBRIETY`: the translated labels spell out the environmental impact of digital technology rather than relying on the less transparent literal “digital sobriety”. The definitions retain all four examples in the French source.
- `SA_DESTINATION_PARTNER_AUDIT`: “réceptifs” is interpreted as inbound tour operators (operadores receptivos / Incoming-Reiseveranstalter) in tourism context. If the source includes destination management companies more broadly, those labels could be broadened after owner review.
- `SA_LABELLED_LOCAL_OFFERS`: “engagés” is interpreted as having sustainability commitments in the parent category context, without claiming certification for every provider. The definition preserves the alternative between commitment and a label.
- `SA_RENEWABLE_ELECTRICITY`: the source's “ou équivalente” remains an equivalent arrangement; no guarantee-of-origin or additionality requirement is inferred. The exact intended equivalent remains unspecified in the source.
- `SA_RAINWATER_REUSE`: the labels retain rainwater and greywater. The broader source definition about collection or reuse for suitable purposes is retained without adding permitted uses or treatment requirements.
- `SA_LOCAL_ORGANIC_FAIRTRADE_FOOD`: the label lists local, organic and fair trade; the definition additionally preserves the native source's ecolabelled option. These remain alternatives, not a requirement to meet every criterion.
- `SA_TERRITORY_SUST_INFO`: “accessibilité” is rendered as accessibility / accesibilidad / Barrierefreiheit, taking its common tourism inclusion meaning. If physical access to the destination is intended instead, German “Erreichbarkeit” would be preferable; no change is made to the French source.
- `SA_PRO_TRANSITION_SUPPORT`: German “Betriebe” makes the tourism-business context explicit for “professionnels”. If individual practitioners are intended as well, “Fachleute und Betriebe” is the proposed broader alternative.
